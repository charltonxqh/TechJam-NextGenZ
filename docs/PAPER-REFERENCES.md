## 1. Hierarchical Group-wise Ranking Framework for Recommendation Models
**arXiv:2506.12756** · https://arxiv.org/html/2506.12756v1

### Problem
Ranking losses rely on in-batch negative sampling, which surfaces mostly *easy*
negatives. Hard-negative mining normally needs expensive real-time retrieval.

### Method
1. **Residual vector quantization (RVQ)** of user embeddings into `L` hierarchical
   discrete codes `c_u = [c_u,1 ... c_u,L]`. Each stage quantizes the residual left by
   the previous stage, so users sharing a longer code prefix are more similar.
2. **Hierarchical group-wise loss** — group user-item pairs by shared code prefix at
   each level and apply a listwise cross-entropy within each group. Shallow levels give
   easy negatives (loosely similar users), deep levels give hard ones. Levels are
   balanced by uncertainty weighting.
3. **Multi-objective training**:
   `L = L_logloss + λ·L_logloss(quantized, via straight-through) + L_hierarchical`

### Reported results (KuaiRand)
| | LogLoss | AUC | GAUC |
|---|---|---|---|
| baseline | 0.5735 | 0.7510 | 0.6911 |
| GroupCE | 0.5681 | 0.7556 | **0.6953** |

**Cold-start users (≤20 impressions): GAUC 0.6718 → 0.6786** — a larger gain than the
overall figure.

### Relevance to us
- ⚠️ **Their GAUC (0.691) is far above our 0.661.** Almost certainly a different task
  setup — they report LogLoss and plain AUC, the signature of click prediction, and
  likely a larger KuaiRand variant with richer features. **But this is unverified, and
  it undermines any confident claim that ~0.66 is the ceiling.**
- Their negatives come from *similar users*; our metric only ever compares items within
  ONE user, so the mechanism partially mismatches our scoring.
- ✅ **The cold-start finding is directly actionable** and independently corroborates our
  own EDA: short-history users are our weakest segment (primary 0.5940 vs 0.6071 for
  medium-history users).

## 2. MMRF: Model-Based Multi-Agent Short-Video Recommendation
**arXiv:2405.01847** · https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2405.01847v1

### Problem
Two issues in industrial short-video ranking: optimising several engagement metrics at
once, and **sample selection bias** — training data only contains feedback for items the
old policy chose to show.

### Method
- **Multi-agent RL.** One agent per engagement metric (watch-time, follow, like,
  comment), each with actor + critic, watch-time as the primary agent. Deterministic
  policy gradient over continuous actions.
- **Agents collaborate via attention** — each computes attention weights over the others'
  hidden representations rather than acting independently.
- **Selection-bias mitigation:** an RNN feedback simulator predicts user responses for
  *non-impression* samples; Monte-Carlo dropout gives uncertainty, and simulated rewards
  are down-weighted by it: `w(s,a) = exp(-σ²(s,a))`.

### Reported results
- **KuaiRand-1K: +7.3% GAUC** on watch-time, +7.1% NCIS.
- Sustained online gains over 13 days in production at hundreds of millions of users.

### Relevance to us
- ❌ The RL machinery (actor-critic, simulator, multi-agent attention) is far beyond what
  a ~0.001-scale effect on 1.14M rows justifies, and it needs an environment we don't have.
- ❌ Their sample-selection-bias fix targets *non-impression* items. Our task only ever
  ranks **logged impressions**, so that specific correction does not apply.
- ✅ **But the underlying idea does:** our training data is generated by a deployed
  recommender, so exposure is biased. Propensity/exposure correction is a real,
  **untested** direction for us — and KuaiRand ships a randomised-exposure log precisely
  for this.
- ✅ **Watch-time as the primary signal**, not an auxiliary one. We tested `play_time_ms`
  only as an auxiliary head (measured −0.0014). Using watch time as *graded relevance*
  for the ranking target is different and untested.

## 4. AIDE: AI-Driven Exploration in the Space of Code
**arXiv:2502.13138** · https://github.com/WecoAI/aideml — *cited in the challenge's own references*

### The search policy (three branches)
1. **Draft** — while fewer than `N` initial solutions exist, generate complete new
   solutions from scratch.
2. **Debug** — if buggy nodes exist within a debug-depth limit, repair them.
3. **Improve** — otherwise apply one *atomic* change to the best working solution.

Stated principle: *"first exploring a set of diverse initial solutions and continuously
improving the best one."*

### Results (MLE-bench, pass@1)
| agent | medal rate | valid submissions |
|---|---|---|
| **AIDE** | **8.7%** | 54.9% |
| OpenHands (linear) | 4.4% | 52.0% |
| MLAB | 0.8% | 44.3% |

With a stronger model AIDE reached 16.9%, *"nearly four times that of the follow-up agent."*

### Relevance to us — high
Our agent is a **greedy single-chain searcher**: it always improves the current best and
never drafts diverse alternatives. The paper names our exact symptom — linear agents
*"tended to terminate early or struggle with iterative refinement"* — and our runs
converge at 3–4 iterations out of 50 every time.

**This is the highest-value unbuilt improvement.** See [[Critical-Review]].

## 5. CWM: Counteracting Duration Bias via Counterfactual Watch Time
**KDD 2024** · https://github.com/hyz20/CWM — *cited by the starter kit*

A completed play means true watch time was **right-censored** by the video's length, so
squared error on watch time is the wrong likelihood; CWM uses a one-sided censored loss.

**Status: never tested by us.** Listed as untested direction #4 in the starter kit's own
README. We tested `play_time_ms` only as an auxiliary BCE/MSE head, which is *not* the
same thing. Its repo pins `torch==1.6.0` and will not install on Apple Silicon — the idea
would have to be reimplemented, not cloned.

## Honest summary of what the survey changes

| claim I made earlier | status after reading |
|---|---|
| "~0.66 is the practical ceiling on KuaiRand" | ⚠️ **Overstated.** Based on one data point (Crocodile). A second paper reports 0.6911/0.6953. Probably a different task, but unverified. |
| "Ranking-loss methods are exhausted here" | ⚠️ Weakened — paper 1 gains from a *better-negatives* ranking loss, a variant we did not test. |
| "Watch-time signal is a dead end" | ❌ **Wrong.** We tested it as an auxiliary head only. Graded relevance and censored regression are untested. |
| "Exposure/propensity bias is out of scope" | ❌ Never tested. Paper 2 motivates it, and KuaiRand ships a randomised log for exactly this. |

**Untested and cheap, in priority order:**
1. Watch-time as **graded relevance** / censored regression (CWM) — organizers' own #4.
2. **Exposure-bias correction** using item exposure frequency as a propensity proxy
   (rules-safe; avoids the `log_random` date-overlap issue).
3. **Cold-start / short-history users** — our weakest segment, corroborated by paper 1.
4. **AIDE drafting phase** for the agent itself.

None of these needs API quota to evaluate.
